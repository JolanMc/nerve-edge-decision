## At a glance

- **Classifier.** INT8 accuracy 98.81 % on the scene-held-out test split (FP32 98.86 %, -0.05 points); the two agree on 99.95 % of the 6,411 test chips. Parameters: 144,874 bytes in INT8 vs 558,728 in FP32.
- **Unusual inputs.** 10 of the 16 simulated sensor faults cost the INT8 classifier at least 3 accuracy points. On those, scores built on the softmax output do worse than chance (mean AUROC 0.437 for MSP, the best of the three): a corrupted chip tends to get a more confident output. The full Nerve Edge Decision score reaches 0.820 and the lite score 0.782; radiometry alone, the simplest component, reaches 0.795 because exposure faults weigh heavily in this benchmark, and Mahalanobis alone 0.630. Far-OOD inputs (CIFAR-10): 0.891 (lite). No signal reaches 0.7 on strong blur (best AUROC 0.55, Deep k-NN) or strong cloud (best AUROC 0.64, Mahalanobis).
- **Bit flips.** 98.7 % of single random flips change no prediction at all. None of the 2,231 uniform trials whose flips all fell in the INT8 weights had a major impact (more than 1 % of predictions changed), with up to 256 flipped bits; all 166 major-impact trials flipped at least one bit of an INT32 bias. The 1,288 bytes of biases (0.9 % of the parameter memory) are where hardening pays in this network. A flip in bits 24–31 of a bias has a major impact in 40.0 % of cases (accuracy down to 64.8 %); in those cases the online layer bounds flag 98.8 % of single inferences without a reference copy of the weights, one golden input catches 100.0 %, and a CRC-32 scrub catches every flip in protected memory by construction. Flipping the sign bit of an INT8 weight had a major impact in 0 of 500 trials.
- **Cost.** INT8 inference is 1.9× faster than FP32 at batch 1 on this CPU. The lite monitor reads only the input chip and the pooled features the network computes anyway; its effect on latency is within measurement noise (+1.5 % at batch 1, -2.4 % per chip at batch 512). Its decision step is 33,024 multiply-accumulates (0.29 % of the network), 256 bound checks and one pass over the 1,200 input values; its state is 67,692 bytes (33,644 with a float16 precision matrix and 8-bit bounds). The full monitor's 320 per-channel maxima cost +42.9 % at batch 1 and +38.8 % per chip at batch 512 on ONNX Runtime, whose separate ReduceMax operators re-read the 41,600 values the convolutions write; computed inside the convolution kernels they would be 41,600 comparisons per chip (0.36 % of the network's multiply-accumulates; not measured here).
